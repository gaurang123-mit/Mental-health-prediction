About Growing Mental Stress

Growing mental stress refers to a gradual increase in emotional, psychological, and physical strain caused by daily pressures such as work or academic demands, social challenges, lifestyle changes, and personal circumstances. Over time, stress may affect mood, concentration, sleep patterns, motivation, and social interactions. Identifying early signs of stress helps in taking timely steps toward self-care, professional support, and healthier coping strategies, ultimately improving overall mental well-being.

📌 Definitions of Each Input Feature

Gender

The biological or self-identified gender of the individual, which may influence emotional experiences and stress patterns.

Country

The country where the individual resides, as cultural, social, and economic factors can affect mental health.

Occupation

The individual’s current role (student or professional), which may contribute to stress through workload and responsibilities.

Self Employed

Indicates whether the individual works independently, often involving irregular income or longer working hours.

Family History

Shows whether mental health conditions exist in close family members, increasing vulnerability to stress.

Treatment

Indicates whether the individual has previously sought or is currently receiving mental health treatment.

Days Indoors

Represents the amount of time spent indoors, as limited outdoor activity and social exposure can affect mood and stress.

Changes in Habits

Refers to noticeable changes in daily routines such as sleep, eating, or activity levels.

Mental Health History

Indicates whether the individual has experienced mental health challenges in the past.

Mood Swings

Frequent or intense emotional changes that may be a sign of emotional imbalance or stress.

Coping Struggles

Shows difficulty in managing stress, emotions, or challenging situations effectively.

Work Interest

Reflects the individual’s level of interest or motivation toward work or academic activities.

Social Weakness

Indicates discomfort or difficulty in social interactions, which can contribute to feelings of isolation.

Mental Health Interview

Shows whether the individual has participated in a mental health assessment or professional consultation.

Care Options

Indicates awareness of or access to mental health support services such as therapy, counseling, or wellness programs.
